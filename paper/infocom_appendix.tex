\section{General-Capacity Impossibility via $\beta$-Mixing}
\label{app:beta}
Corollary~\ref{cor:imp} showed that feedback's value vanishes with the sensing age
$D$ for the two-state model. We now show the same for any stationary capacity
process, with the decay rate governed by its $\beta$-mixing coefficient, also
called absolute regularity. The exact binary law is one tight instance of that
general statement.

\noindent\textbf{Setup.}
Let $\{a_t\}$ be stationary on $\{0,\dots,C\}$ with
$\mathcal F_{\le s}=\sigma(a_u:u\le s)$ and $\mathcal F_{\ge s}=\sigma(a_u:u\ge s)$.
Its $\beta$-mixing coefficient at lag $D$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
\beta(D)=\mathbb E\!\left[\ \sup_{B\in\mathcal F_{\ge t}}\big|\mathbb P(B\mid\mathcal F_{\le t-D})-\mathbb P(B)\big|\ \right],
\end{equation}
and $\beta(D)\!\to\!0$ holds if and only if the process is absolutely regular.
Geometrically mixing processes satisfy $\beta(D)\le c_0\varrho^{D}$ for some
$\varrho\in(0,1)$. Note that $\beta(D)$ always carries its lag argument and is
unrelated to the AIMD back-off factor $\beta$. Fix any bounded per-epoch objective
$R(w,a)$, for instance the politeness Lagrangian
$R_\lambda(w,a)=\min(w,a)-\lambda(w-a)^{+}$, with oscillation
$\Omega\triangleq\max_{w,a}R-\min_{w,a}R$. A causal age-$D$ controller sets $w_t$
measurable with respect to $\mathcal G\triangleq\mathcal F_{\le t-D}$, while a
fixed controller uses a $\mathcal G$-independent $w$. Write $V^\star(D)$ and
$V_{\mathrm{stat}}$ for their optimal per-epoch values and
$\Delta(D)=V^\star(D)-V_{\mathrm{stat}}$.

\begin{lemma}[Lipschitz value function]\label{lem:lip}
$\psi(\mu)\triangleq\max_w\sum_a R(w,a)\,\mu(a)$ is convex on the simplex and
$\Omega$-Lipschitz in total variation, so
$|\psi(\mu)-\psi(\mu')|\le\Omega\,\|\mu-\mu'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:beta} below is then the standard value-of-information
bound~\cite{howard1966,delaragossner} specialized to the mixing coefficient.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\psi$ is a pointwise maximum of linear functionals, hence convex. Let $w_\mu$
attain $\psi(\mu)$. Then $\psi(\mu)-\psi(\mu')\le\sum_a R(w_\mu,a)\,(\mu-\mu')(a)\le
\mathrm{osc}_a R(w_\mu,\cdot)\,\|\mu-\mu'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\Omega\|\mu-\mu'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$,
using $\sum_a f(a)\nu(a)\le\mathrm{osc}(f)\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ for zero-sum
$\nu$. The reverse bound is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\beta$-mixing impossibility]\label{thm:beta}
For any stationary capacity process and any bounded objective,
\begin{equation}
0\ \le\ \Delta(D)\ \le\ \Omega\,\beta(D).
\end{equation}
Hence no causal age-$D$ controller outperforms a fixed budget as $D\to\infty$
whenever the capacity is absolutely regular. If $\beta(D)\le c_0\varrho^{D}$ the
advantage decays geometrically, with $\Delta(D)\le\Omega c_0\varrho^{D}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mu_{\mathcal G}=\mathbb P(a_t\in\cdot\mid\mathcal G)$, so the tower property
gives $\mathbb E[\mu_{\mathcal G}]=\bar\mu\triangleq\mathbb P(a_t\in\cdot)$.
Optimizing the $\mathcal G$-measurable window pointwise,
$V^\star(D)=\mathbb E[\psi(\mu_{\mathcal G})]$ and
$V_{\mathrm{stat}}=\psi(\bar\mu)$, so convexity of $\psi$ and Jensen give
$\Delta(D)=\mathbb E[\psi(\mu_{\mathcal G})]-\psi(\bar\mu)\ge0$, meaning fresher
information never hurts. For the upper bound, Lemma~\ref{lem:lip} gives
$\Delta(D)\le\Omega\,\mathbb E\|\mu_{\mathcal G}-\bar\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\Omega\,\mathbb E[\sup_{B}|\mathbb P(a_t{\in}B\mid\mathcal G)-\mathbb P(a_t{\in}B)|]
\le\Omega\beta(D)$. The last step holds because
$\sigma(a_t)\subseteq\mathcal F_{\ge t}$, so \eqref{eq:beta} takes the supremum
over a larger field.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Binary tightness]\label{cor:tight}
For the symmetric two-state model, $\beta(D)=\tfrac12|1-2q(D)|=\tfrac12 r(D)$ by a
standard mixing identity~\cite{bradley2005,lpw}, so Thm.~\ref{thm:beta}
reproduces the exact advantage of Thm.~\ref{thm:limit} up to the objective scale
$\Omega$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The Markov property reduces $\mathcal F_{\le t-D}$ to $a_{t-D}$. The
conditional-to-marginal total-variation distance is $|\tfrac12-q(D)|$ from either
state, so $\beta(D)=\tfrac12-q(D)=\tfrac12 r(D)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Learning Rate of Algorithm~\ref{alg:adaptive}}
\label{app:regret}
We give both directions of Thm.~\ref{thm:adapt}. Observations are delayed but
clean, so $\hat a_i=a_{i-D}$, and Algorithm~\ref{alg:adaptive} forms
$\hat p_n=\tfrac1n\sum_{i\le n}\mathbf 1[\hat a_i\neq\hat a_{i-1}]$ and
$\hat r_n=(1-2\hat p_n)^{D}$.

Since $\Pr[a_t\neq a_{t-1}\mid a_0,\dots,a_{t-1}]=p$ for every history, $n\hat p_n$
is exactly Binomial$(n,p)$, with variance $p(1-p)/n$ and per-sample Fisher
information $1/(p(1-p))$. A generic reversible-chain Hoeffding bound would replace
that variance by a range proxy of order $1/\gamma$, overstating the sample
requirement by order $1/\gamma^{2}$ and inverting the sign of the $\gamma$
dependence, so we use the Bernstein form of \cite{paulin2015}, which is stated in
the asymptotic variance and is exact here.

\noindent\textbf{Upper bound.}
Bernstein gives $|\hat p_n-p|=O(\sigma_\theta\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n})$ with
probability $1-\delta$. The map $p\mapsto(1-2p)^{D}$ has derivative $s_\theta$ at
$p_\theta$, so $|\hat r_n-r(D)|=O(s_\theta\sigma_\theta\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n})$.
A wrong branch costs at most $L$ times the shortfall in $r$, and summing while $n$
grows, then dividing by the horizon, gives $O(L s_\theta\sigma_\theta/\sqrt N)$.

\noindent\textbf{Lower bound.}
Take two processes with flip probabilities $p_\theta\pm\delta/2$. Independence of
the flip indicators makes the Kullback-Leibler divergence between $n$-sample laws
$n\delta^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}_\theta)+O(n\delta^{3})$, so no test separates them with
constant probability unless $n\gtrsim\sigma^{2}_\theta/\delta^{2}$. Their
correlations differ by $s_\theta\delta$ and the loss is locally \emph{linear} in
$r-\theta$ at the indifference level, so a rule that cannot distinguish them pays
$\Omega(L s_\theta\delta)$ on one. Le Cam's two-point
argument~\cite{tsybakov} with $\delta=\sigma_\theta/\sqrt N$ matches the upper
bound. Local linearity pins the exponent at $N^{-1/2}$, where a loss quadratic in
$r-\theta$ would give $N^{-1}$. The two flip probabilities differ by
$O(N^{-1/2})$, so their spectral gaps agree to that order and the pair certifies
the rate without invoking a class boundary. That matters because $\theta$ is fixed
by the objective, so on the hard manifold $r(D)=\theta$ the quantities $D$,
$\gamma_\theta=1-\theta^{1/D}$ and $s_\theta$ are mutually determined. Treating
them as free is what yields a spurious $1/\sqrt{\gamma_0 N}$ rate.

\noindent\textbf{Worst switch level.}
With $u=1-2p$ and $\theta=u^{D}$ we have
$s_\theta\sigma_\theta=D\,u^{D-1}\sqrt{1-u^{2}}$. Setting the derivative of
$(D-1)\log u+\tfrac12\log(1-u^{2})$ to zero gives $u^{2}=(D-1)/D$, so
$\sqrt{1-u^{2}}=1/\sqrt D$ and $u^{D-1}\to e^{-1/2}$. The product is
$e^{-1/2}\sqrt D$ at $\theta\to e^{-1/2}$, which is \eqref{eq:sup}.
